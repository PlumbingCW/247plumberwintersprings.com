Water Softener and Filtration in Winter Springs, FL

Chalky showerheads, spotted glasses and a short-lived water heater all trace back to the same minerals. Here is how softening and filtering work.

A water softener and filtration system treats the water entering your house so minerals and tastes do not reach the taps. Our licensed plumbers size, install and repair these systems in Winter Springs, FL, and we tell you what each one does and does not do.

Softeners and filters solve different problems, and homeowners often buy one when they need the other. Hardness is scale. Odor, color and chlorine taste are filtration questions. We start by asking what you see in the house, then match equipment to it.

The city utility lists Winter Springs hardness at an average of 150 mg/L, which it states as 8 grains. On the USGS hardness scale, 121 to 180 mg/L is hard water. Calcium and magnesium come out of solution when water is heated, and they coat heating elements, tank bottoms and the inside of pipes.

That scale shortens water heater life and clogs aerators, according to the city. White crust on faucets and spots on dishes are the visible signs. A softener removes the hardness minerals before the water reaches the heater, which is why most installs go at the point where water enters the house.

How a softener works

The mechanism is simple, which makes repairs predictable.

Resin tank

Water passes through tiny resin beads that hold sodium. Calcium and magnesium swap places with the sodium and stay on the beads.

Regeneration

When the beads are full, the control valve flushes them with brine from the salt tank, then rinses the brine to drain.

Control valve

The valve sets timing and flow. It is the part that fails most often, with symptoms like hard water coming back or water running to drain nonstop.

Bypass

A valve set that lets you route water around the softener during service, so the house keeps running.

Sulfur odor, irrigation wells and filters

The city says sulfur odor and orange stains in some homes come from shallow irrigation-well groundwater, not from reclaimed water. The city utility treats its own supply by aeration to remove hydrogen sulfide, then adds liquid chlorine.

The city also notes that point-of-entry filters remove chlorine, which can make natural hydrogen sulfide odor more noticeable. That is a tradeoff worth knowing before you install a whole-house carbon filter. If a smell appears after you add a filter, call us. We check which tap, hot or cold, and whether your water comes from the city or a private well.

Whole-home filtration choices

A whole-home system treats the water at the point where it enters the house, so every faucet, shower and appliance benefits. Typical stages include a sediment cartridge, a carbon block for taste and chlorine, and sometimes a separate unit for iron or sulfur. Each stage has a life and a flow rating.

Pick the system by problem, not by brochure. A softener will not remove chlorine, and a carbon filter will not stop scale. Reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink handles drinking water only, and it wastes some water to drain. If you are replumbing an area anyway, see repiping for how treatment equipment fits into a new layout.

Repairing a softener or conditioner

Most service calls are for the control valve, a salt bridge, or a clogged injector. A salt bridge is a crust that forms in the brine tank and leaves a gap under it, so the unit looks full but makes no brine. Breaking the crust and cleaning the tank often fixes the problem.

If hard water returns, check the salt level and the time on the head first. If the valve runs continuously or leaks at the drain, call us. We also replace worn resin, though a unit older than the resin's life is often cheaper to replace than rebuild. A softener that connects to your water heater line protects the tank from scale, so a failed unit shows up there first.

What affects the cost of treatment equipment

System type

A basic softener, a multi-stage whole-home filter and an iron filter are different products.

Capacity

Household size, bathrooms and flow demand set the resin and tank size.

Installation layout

Distance to the drain, electrical outlet and access to the main line change labor.

Water test results

If testing finds iron or sulfur, extra stages may be needed.

Ongoing supplies

Salt, cartridges and filter media are recurring costs you should budget for.

Insurance note

A resin tank that cracks and floods the utility room is sudden damage a policy may cover. Scale, a worn control valve or a salt bridge is ordinary wear, and the unit itself is usually not covered. Ask your insurer about your policy.
