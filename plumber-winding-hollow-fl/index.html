Winding Hollow is a community of single-family homes in Winter Springs, off State Road 434, just west of Tuskawilla Road, close to the Winter Springs Town Center. Listing sites show a homeowners association, a neighborhood pond and lots of roughly a quarter to a half acre.

Most homes here date from the late 1990s, and many have pools and irrigated yards. That puts a lot of plumbing outside the house. This page covers leaks you cannot see, irrigation and water line problems, and the indoor repairs that follow.

Reclaimed water outside, city water inside

The city treats wastewater at two plants and sells the reclaimed water for irrigation. Its reclaimed water page says it is used only for lawn irrigation, is not for drinking and can carry more salt and nutrients than drinking water.

Reclaimed lines must stay separate from the lines that feed your faucets. If a hose bib or sprinkler connection puzzles you, ask the city Utility Division before connecting anything. A mix-up between the two is exactly what water line repair work has to prevent.

Finding a leak across a half-acre lot

A leak in a yard line rarely shows up as a puddle in Florida sand. More often the lawn turns greener in one strip, the meter keeps moving or the water bill climbs with no change in habits.

The test starts at the meter. Turn off everything inside, then watch the leak indicator or the dial. If it still moves, the leak is on the supply or irrigation side, and a plumber can isolate the section by section.

Irrigation hours and why they matter to your plumbing

The city's reclaimed water page lists these watering limits. Schedules change, so check the page before you set a timer.

Timers that run on the wrong source or the wrong days waste water and hide leaks. Compare your timer to the city's current schedule.

Pools add a second plumbing system

A pool has its own fill line, return lines, valves and equipment pad. When the pool level drops faster than evaporation explains, or the equipment pad stays wet, a plumber can pressure test the lines before anyone digs.

A tank water heater from 1998 is long gone in most homes, so the one you have now is probably a replacement. Tank heaters often last roughly eight to twelve years, and water heater replacement is a planned job when you catch it early.

Questions to answer before a plumber arrives

These help narrow a yard or pool leak quickly.

  • ✓Has your water bill gone up with no change in use?
  • ✓Does the meter move when every tap and the irrigation are off?
  • ✓Is one strip of lawn greener or soggier than the rest?
  • ✓Is your pool level dropping faster than it used to?
  • ✓Do you know whether your irrigation runs on reclaimed or city water?
