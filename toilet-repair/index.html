A toilet that is overflowing, leaking at the base or refilling without stopping needs attention now, and our 24/7 dispatch takes the call at any hour. While you wait, turn the oval supply valve on the wall behind the bowl clockwise until it stops, and do not flush again.

Toilet repair in Winter Springs, FL means finding which part has failed, from the fill valve and flapper inside the tank to the wax seal and flange under the bowl. Our licensed plumbers fix the part, or replace the whole toilet when the porcelain or the floor connection is past saving.

What to do while the bowl is still rising

Most toilet floods are small until someone flushes one more time. These steps keep the damage to the bathroom floor and nothing else.

  • ✓Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water entering the bowl.
  • ✓Close the supply valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  • ✓Stop flushing and tell everyone in the house to use another bathroom.
  • ✓Soak up standing water with towels so it does not reach the subfloor edge.
  • ✓Do not pour chemical drain opener into a full bowl, because it can splash and burn skin.
  • ✓If water is near outlets or a floor heater, cut that circuit at the panel only if the panel is dry and reachable without standing in water. Otherwise call.

Toilet repair or a new toilet

Most failures are cheap parts. A fill valve wears out, a flapper curls, a flush lever snaps off its arm, or a supply line starts to weep at the nut. Those jobs take a short visit, and the toilet stays in place.

Replacement makes sense when the bowl or tank is cracked, when the toilet has needed three repairs in a year, or when you are remodeling and want a different height or flush rating. A hairline crack in porcelain cannot be sealed in any lasting way.

When we install a new toilet we set a fresh wax ring or a rubber seal, check that the flange sits level with the finished floor, and tighten the closet bolts evenly. Over-tightening one side is how new bowls crack, so we alternate between the two nuts a little at a time, then run the first flushes ourselves and watch the base.

A toilet that runs on its own

A running toilet looks harmless, but it can waste a surprising amount of water over a month. Three parts cause nearly all of it.

The flapper

The rubber disc at the bottom of the tank hardens or warps and stops sealing. Drop a little food coloring in the tank, and if color reaches the bowl without a flush, the flapper is leaking.

The fill valve

When the valve does not shut off at the right water level, water spills down the overflow tube. You will hear a steady hiss or a refill every few minutes.

The overflow tube and chain

A chain that is too short holds the flapper slightly open, and a tube cut too low lets water slip over the top. Both are quick adjustments once found.

A leak at the base of the toilet

Water around the foot of a toilet has three common sources, and the fix is different for each. Condensation sweating off a cold tank drips down the outside and looks like a leak. A loose supply connection drips onto the floor from above. A failed wax seal leaks from underneath, usually when the toilet is flushed.

A wax ring seals the drain outlet of the bowl to the closet flange, which is the ring screwed to the floor that connects to the drain pipe. If the flange is cracked or sits below the floor line, a new wax ring alone will not hold, and we add a flange repair ring or a spacer.

Soft or stained flooring around the base is a sign the leak has been running for a while.

When several fixtures back up together

If the toilet gurgles when the shower runs, or water rises in the tub when you flush, the clog is probably not in the toilet at all. A blockage in the main line backs up through the lowest fixtures first. The city lists 112 miles of gravity sewer main in its wastewater system, and a stoppage on your side of the connection shows up at home this way.

Plunging will not fix it and can push waste into the tub. Stop using water, and call us. We will confirm whether it is a single-fixture clog or a line problem and point you to sewer line cleaning when the main needs clearing.

What changes the price of a toilet repair

We give a clear price before work starts, after we have looked at the toilet and the shutoff. These are the things that move the number.

Part or full replacement

A flapper or fill valve is a small job. A new toilet adds the fixture itself, the seal, and haul-away of the old one.

Condition of the flange and floor

A broken flange or soft subfloor adds repair work before the toilet can be set again.

Supply valve and line

Old multi-turn valves that seize shut are often replaced at the same visit so you can isolate the toilet next time.

Access

A toilet in a tight closet or a second-floor bathroom takes longer to remove and carry out.

Time of day

Insurance note

An overflow that soaks the bathroom floor and the room below is the sudden kind of damage a homeowner policy may cover. A flapper or wax ring that simply wore out is not. Ask your insurer before you decide, and keep photos of any damage.
