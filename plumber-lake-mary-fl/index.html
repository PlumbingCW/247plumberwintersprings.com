Tankless water heaters in Lake Mary homes

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so it needs a gas line or electric circuit sized for the load and a vent that suits the model. Those are the details that decide whether a swap goes smoothly. A unit that is too small for the number of showers running together will cool off mid-rinse.

Our tankless water heater work covers replacing a tank with a tankless unit, servicing one that errors out and flushing scale from the heat exchanger. We check the rated gas input on the unit against the line feeding it before anything is promised.

Tank or tankless, in plain terms

Neither type is right for every house. This table shows the trade-offs we talk through.

QuestionTank heaterTankless heater
How does it supply hot water?Heats a stored volume and reheats as you use itHeats on demand as water passes through
What happens when the power or gas stops?Stored hot water is still there for a short timeHot water stops with the fuel or power
What does it need?Floor space, a drain pan and a relief lineProperly sized gas or power and a correct vent
What maintenance matters?Flushing the tank, checking the anodeDescaling the heat exchanger on a schedule

Permit rules vary, so ask your local building department before a swap.

Water treatment: what a softener or filter can and cannot do

Lake Mary's city water comes from 5 Floridan wells. It is treated with UV advanced oxidation using hydrogen peroxide, then granular activated carbon and chlorination. Read the city's current water report and tell us what it lists.

Hardness is a separate question, so tell us the figure the report gives. A water softener or filtration system can reduce scale and filter taste or odor, but we never promise results, because water chemistry varies by street and by season.

Point-of-entry filters can remove chlorine, which also makes natural sulfur odors easier to notice in some homes.

How a tankless swap gets planned

A tankless install fails or succeeds on planning. These are the checks we make before quoting the work.

  1. 1

    Read the unit's nameplate

    The rated gas input or electrical load tells us what the supply must deliver at full burn.

  2. 2

    Check the existing line

    We look at the pipe size and run length feeding the old heater, since a long or narrow line can starve a larger burner.

  3. 3

    Plan the vent

    Condensing and non-condensing units vent differently, and the route from the unit to outside has to suit the model.

  4. 4

    Check the water side

    We look at supply pressure, shutoff valves and the drain path, and add service valves so flushing is easy later.

  5. 5

    Agree the price

    You hear the price and the scope before any work begins.

After-hours heater failures

A heater can fail at any hour, and a failure does not wait for the office to open. A pooling puddle, a rotten smell near a gas unit or a tripped breaker should not wait for office hours.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. For a water leak or a dead heater, our 24/7 line is the right call. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and the price comes first.

A tank that has leaked for days may need replacement rather than repair. We check the fittings and the tank seam and say which it is.

Before we arrive in Lake Mary

A failed heater or a burst line needs these steps at any hour.

1

Shut the main valve

Turn the main off at the meter or where the line enters the house. For a heater leak, close its cold-water inlet valve if you can reach it.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Turn off an electric heater at its breaker. For a gas heater, set the control to off. Do not touch an electric panel while standing in water.

3

Clear the area

Move boxes and electronics away from the heater and any wet floor. Put a bucket under drips.
