Plumbing for Winter Springs Town Center, FL

The Town Center is a commercial and mixed-use district, so tenant lines, shared drains and backflow devices come up as often as leaky faucets.

The Winter Springs Town Center sits at State Road 434 and Tuskawilla Road, and it is a commercial and mixed-use district rather than a residential subdivision. It opened in 2002 and is the city's planned downtown, with shops, restaurants and offices.

This page is for businesses and property managers there, and for neighbors who shop and eat nearby. For commercial plumbing, restaurant drains and backflow questions, call (407) 964-8940.

Winter Springs Town Center at a glance

Where it sits

State Road 434 at Tuskawilla Road, in the middle of Winter Springs, per the city.

What it is

A planned downtown with retail, restaurants and offices. City plans also call for hotel and residential uses at build-out.

Events

The city holds its annual Winter Wonderland parade here, and Blumberg Boulevard hosts weddings, special events and the Festival of the Arts.

Next to it

Homes nearby are covered on the Winding Hollow and Tuscawilla pages.

A downtown that was planned, not grown

The city says the Town Center was designed around traditional streets, squares and buildings set close to the road. At full build-out its plans call for retail, office, hotel and residential space, though part of the site is still undeveloped.

For plumbing, the mix matters. A restaurant, an office suite and a rental apartment can share a building, and each has different loads and different people responsible for the pipes.

Restaurants, grease and drains under heavy use

Kitchen drains carry fat, starch and soap that cool and cling to pipe walls. Grease interceptors catch much of it but only when they are cleaned on schedule, and a full interceptor sends the problem downstream.

Slow floor drains, gurgling fixtures and odors after closing are early signs. Cleaning the line before it blocks costs less than a Saturday night stoppage. Hydro jetting clears grease from pipe walls in a way a snake alone often cannot.

Backflow devices and the city rules

The city's cross-connection control rules require backflow devices with test cocks to be tested once a year. Only certified testers are accepted, the customer pays for testing and repairs, and the results go to the city Utility Department.

Hospitals need a reduced pressure device on the main service line, and fire sprinkler lines use a double check valve assembly. Commercial owners should ask the Utility Division which device applies to their service.

Event weekends and after-hours leaks

The city says a typical Town Center event draws 4,000 to 5,000 people. Restrooms and drains in nearby businesses take a heavy load on those days, so check flush valves, drains and shutoffs ahead of the date.

Vacant units are the other risk. A leak in an empty space can run for days before anyone notices, so ask the property manager to check meters and shutoff valves in empty suites on a regular schedule.

Permits, digging and the city process

Permit rules vary, so ask the Winter Springs building department. The city's permit application treats plumbing as a separate permit and asks for the number of plumbing fixtures and a contractor license number.

Any digging for a water or sewer line calls for an 811 ticket at least two full business days ahead. Plan that lead time into a tenant build-out or a line repair.
