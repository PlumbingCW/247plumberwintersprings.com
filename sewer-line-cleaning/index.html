Sewer line cleaning in Winter Springs, FL is the clearing of the main pipe that carries all of a home's wastewater out to the street connection. A sewage backup is urgent, and our dispatch runs 24/7. Until a plumber arrives, stop using water, do not flush, and keep people and pets out of the wet area.

If the water is near outlets or appliances, leave the power off in that room. Wastewater is not safe to wade through or wipe up bare-handed.

Sewage coming up right now: do this first

Stop all water use. That means no showers, no laundry, no dishwasher and no flushing. Every gallon you send down adds to what is already surfacing.

Find the main water shutoff and close it if overflow continues. Move rugs and belongings off the floor. Open windows if the smell is strong, and call the 24/7 line.

Do not try to snake the main line yourself with a rented machine unless you know where the cleanout is and how large the cable should be. A wrong cable can jam inside the pipe and turn a clog into a repair.

How to tell a sewer backup from a slow sink

A single slow drain is usually a local problem, and our page on drain cleaning covers it. A sewer backup shows up in more than one place, and the lowest fixture in the house gives way first.

Typical signs are a tub that fills when the toilet is flushed, a floor drain that gurgles when the washing machine runs, and a toilet that bubbles when the shower drains. Gray or dark water rising in a shower pan is a sure sign.

These clues point to the main line because every branch joins it. When the main is restricted, water has nowhere to go and finds the lowest exit.

What blocks a main sewer line

Roots are the best known cause. They enter at joints and grow into a mat that catches paper and grease. Grease is next, since it cools on the pipe wall and thickens over months. Wipes and paper towels flushed down the toilet pile up at bends.

Structural problems also stop lines. A sagging section holds standing water and solids. A cracked or offset joint lets soil in. A cable or camera shows which cause applies, and the cause decides the fix.

Cleaning restores flow. It does not repair a broken pipe. If the camera finds one, we explain the next step, which may be sewer line repair.

How a main line gets cleared

The work follows a set order so the blockage is cleared and the cause is found.

  1. 1

    Locate the cleanout

    Most homes have a capped cleanout outside near the foundation. If there is none, we work from a pulled toilet or the roof vent.

  2. 2

    Open the line with a machine cable

    A heavy cable with a cutting head breaks up roots and grease, usually through a 3 or 4 inch pipe.

  3. 3

    Flush and test

    We run water to confirm the line drains at full volume, not just a trickle.

  4. 4

    Look with a camera when the cause is unclear

    A small camera shows the pipe condition, and we point out what we see.

  5. 5

    Talk through next steps

    Some lines need only cleaning. Others benefit from jetting or repair, and we say which.

Main sewer cleaning and sewer drain cleaning: same job, different names

People search for main sewer cleaning, sewer drain cleaning and main line clog clearing. They describe one job, which is reopening the large pipe at the bottom of the plumbing system.

A clog in the main line does not always back up into the house right away. A partial blockage can pass water slowly for weeks. If the main drain is gurgling, call before it stops completely.

When grease or roots keep returning, hydro jetting washes the full pipe wall instead of opening a channel only.

What a sewer cleaning costs depends on

We give a clear price before work starts, and the following factors set it.

Cleanout access

A cleanout speeds the work. Without one, we pull a fixture, which adds labor.

Distance to the blockage

A clog near the house is faster than one far down toward the street.

Blockage type

Roots need cutting heads and more time than soft grease.

Add-ons

A camera look or a jetting pass is extra and is quoted separately.

Hour of the call

Ask whether the hour of the call affects the price. You hear the price before work starts.

Insurance note

If sewage soaks flooring or drywall, that sudden accidental damage is what insurance typically considers. Roots, grease buildup and the cost of clearing the pipe count as wear. Ask your insurer whether your policy includes sewer backup coverage.

If a backup reached floors or walls, take your own photos of the damage before cleanup and keep them for your insurer.
