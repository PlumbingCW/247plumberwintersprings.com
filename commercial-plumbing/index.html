When a business plumbing problem cannot wait

Some problems close the doors. Others can be scheduled for the morning. These are the ones we treat as urgent.

Water running through a ceiling or wall

Shut the building main, cut power to wet circuits if it is safe, and move stock and records. Water moves along framing and shows up far from the source.

Sewage backing out of a floor drain

A restaurant or office with sewage on the floor cannot stay open. Stop all fixtures and call, because every flush adds to the spill.

No water at all

A closed supply valve, a failed pressure regulator or a broken service line can leave a whole building dry.

A failed backflow assembly

A device that leaks or fails its test must be repaired or replaced before the next test deadline, and it may need the supply shut off.

Finding a leak in a commercial building

Commercial leak detection is harder than in a home. Pipes run above drop ceilings, inside chases and under slab floors, and several tenants may share a riser. We use pressure tests on isolated sections, moisture meters and thermal imaging to narrow the search before anyone opens a wall.

We are honest about limits. A camera cannot see inside a slab, and a leak in a shared riser may need the building to shut down a section for testing. We tell you which tool fits, what it can prove, and what the next step is if the result is inconclusive. If the leak is under a slab, see leak detection for how we trace it.

A meter that moves while every fixture is off is a strong sign of a leak somewhere on the property. Note the reading before you call.

Backflow testing for Winter Springs businesses

A business with a fire sprinkler line, an irrigation system or a medical connection usually has a backflow device. The city cross-connection control manual, adopted as Ordinance No. 493 in July 1990, says devices with test cocks must be tested once a year and only certified testers are accepted by the city utility department.

Irrigation systems need an AVB, PVB or DCVA. A DCVA is the type used on fire sprinkler lines, and hospitals need an RP device on main service connections. The customer pays for testing and repair, and results go to the Utility Department.

We repair and replace assemblies, and we coordinate with a certified tester so a failed test does not turn into a missed deadline. For fixture and device installs, see plumbing installation.

Drains, grease and floor sinks

Kitchen drain lines clog with grease and food solids, and a grease trap that is overdue for cleaning sends fat into the building line. We clear stoppages from the kitchen, floor drains and branch lines with cable machines and jetting, and we tell you when pumping the trap is the right fix.

Floor drains and lift pumps in basements or below-grade rooms need attention too. A lift pump that fails during a storm leaves water standing on a floor. We test the float, the check valve and the discharge.

For storm drains and yard drains on a lot, we check the grate and the line for debris and roots. Where a main line is involved, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall rather than punching a hole through the blockage.

Property managers and tenant work orders

Managers need three things from a plumber: fast access coordination, a clear description of the cause, and an explanation they can pass to the owner. Give us the unit, the tenant's reported symptom and who holds the key.

We tell you what failed, what we replaced, and any water damage we saw, so you can separate wear from misuse. Repeat calls on the same unit trigger a look at the cause, not another patch. Multi-unit buildings often share risers and drains, so a stoppage in one unit may need access to the one next to it.
