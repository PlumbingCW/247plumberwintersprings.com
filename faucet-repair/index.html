Faucet repair in Winter Springs, FL covers the fixture itself, the valves that feed it, and the shower or tub controls behind the wall. We find whether the problem is a worn cartridge, a corroded stop or a bad connection, then repair or replace only what has failed.

When a valve breaks and sprays: do this first

A broken supply valve is the faucet problem that cannot wait. Old angle stops seize with mineral scale, then snap or leak at the stem when someone finally turns them. If the stop is spraying and will not close, go to the main shutoff, which is usually at the front of the house or at the meter box.

Once the water is off, open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain the lines, and move anything valuable out of the cabinet. Call us right away. Replacing a supply valve means cutting into the pipe, so it is not a good job for a first attempt with a wrench.

Faucets, fixtures and the valves behind them

The part you see is rarely the whole problem. A faucet sits on a supply line, which connects to an angle stop, which connects to the pipe in the wall. Any of the three can leak, and each needs its own repair.

We check the stop and line whenever we work on the faucet, because replacing a faucet on a corroded stop only moves the leak. If the stop will not hold, we replace it with a quarter-turn valve that you can close firmly next time.

Fixture replacement is part of the work too. If you chose a new kitchen or bathroom faucet, bring it and we set it, check the supply connections and test for leaks at full pressure.

The city lists Winter Springs water at an average of 150 mg/L hardness, which it states as 8 grains. On the USGS scale, 121 to 180 mg/L counts as hard. That mineral content leaves chalky scale inside faucet aerators and around cartridges, so flow drops and handles stiffen.

Soaking a clogged aerator in vinegar clears light buildup. Never combine vinegar and bleach. Rinse the aerator fully between the two. If the cartridge is scaled, it usually needs replacement, since scraping damages the seals. A water softener or filtration system reduces the scale at the source.

Shower and tub controls

Shower valves sit inside the wall, so the symptoms matter more than usual.

Water will not shut off

A worn cartridge or seat lets water through even with the handle off. Closing the main valve stops the drip until we replace the part.

Temperature swings

A pressure-balancing cartridge that has scaled up stops evening out hot and cold when someone flushes elsewhere.

Water leaks to the tub spout while showering

The diverter has worn and lets water leak through to the tub spout. Replacing the diverter or the spout fixes it.

Tub or shower drain backs up

Hair and soap block the strainer and trap first. If other fixtures are also slow, it is a drain line problem, and we move to drain cleaning.

What affects a faucet repair price

The gap between a quick fix and a bigger job comes down to a handful of things.

Repair kit or new faucet

A cartridge or washer swap costs less than a new faucet. Discontinued models often push the decision to replacement.

Supply stops and lines

Seized or leaking stops are replaced as part of the job, which adds parts and time.

Wall access

Shower valves behind tile may need an access panel or a cut in the wall.

Fixture you choose

A basic fixture and a designer one with a pull-down sprayer are very different parts.

After-hours dispatch

Ask whether the hour of the call affects the price. You hear the price before work starts.

Insurance note

A supply valve that bursts and soaks the vanity and the floor is sudden damage a policy may cover. A drip from a worn washer, or the faucet you replace because it is old, is not. Ask your insurer what applies to your policy.
