What comes out of the tap here

The city pumps groundwater from the Floridan Aquifer, aerates it to remove hydrogen sulfide and treats it with liquid chlorine. It lists average hardness at 150 mg/L, or 8 grains.

That level leaves scale on heating elements and burner assemblies and clogs faucet aerators over time. Many Tuscawilla homeowners pair a descaling routine with a water softener or filter to slow it down.

Sprinkler systems and the yearly backflow test

The city's cross-connection control rules require backflow devices with test cocks to be tested once a year. Only certified testers are accepted, the owner pays for testing and repairs, and results go to the city Utility Department.

Lawn sprinkler systems must have a backflow device, such as an atmospheric or pressure vacuum breaker. If you have never had yours tested, ask your irrigation installer which device you have, and ask the city Utility Division for the current rules.

Whose pipe is it, and who do you call?

Water flowing from the street side of your meter is the city's concern. The city lists (407) 327-2669 as its 24-hour line for utility emergencies. Water that appears after the meter, inside your yard or house, is a plumbing call.

Where the city's responsibility ends for your sewer line is a question for the city Utility Division. We do not guess at it. If a main line is backing up, sewer line cleaning starts with finding where the blockage sits.
