Emergency Slab Leak Repair in Winter Springs, FL

A warm patch on the tile, the sound of running water in a silent house or a bill that doubled for no reason. These are the usual first signs of a pipe leaking under the concrete.

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs in or under the concrete foundation of a house, and slab leak repair in Winter Springs, FL starts with proving that is where the water is going. If water is rising through flooring, call our 24/7 dispatch right away.

Until we arrive, shut off the main valve and turn off the water heater's gas supply so it does not fire on an empty tank. For an electric heater, cut power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and reachable without standing in water. Otherwise call. Open a faucet to relieve pressure and keep family and pets off any wet floor.

Warm floors, a constant hiss and other signs the slab is leaking

Concrete hides a leak, but the home usually gives it away. These are the symptoms we hear about most.

A warm or hot spot on the floor

A leaking hot water line heats the concrete above it. The spot stays warm even when no hot water is running.

Running water sound with everything off

A faint hiss or trickle from the floor in a quiet house points to pressurized water escaping.

A meter that keeps moving

With every fixture off, a spinning meter means a leak somewhere between the street and your faucets.

Damp carpet, lifting tile or cracked grout

Water pushing up through the slab loosens flooring and leaves damp patches along baseboards.

Low hot water pressure

Pressure drop on the hot side only often means the hot line is the leaking one.

How we pinpoint the leak before any concrete is cut

Breaking a slab at the wrong spot costs time and money, so locating comes first.

  1. 1

    Isolate the system

    We shut off the water heater and close branch valves to see which line is losing pressure.

  2. 2

    Pressure test the lines

    A gauge on each isolated line shows whether it holds. A line that drops is the leaking one.

  3. 3

    Listen along the floor

    Acoustic sensors pick up the hiss through the concrete. The loudest point marks the likely break.

  4. 4

    Confirm with a second reading

    Thermal imaging or a moisture meter backs up the sound before we cut anything.

Spot repair, reroute or repipe

There are three ways to deal with a slab leak. Which one fits depends on the pipe, the number of leaks and how much of the house you want to disturb.

Why rerouting is often the better call

Cutting a slab is noisy, dusty and messy. A reroute avoids it by capping the leaking line at both ends and running new pipe overhead or through walls to the same fixtures. The slab stays intact and the old pipe is simply abandoned.

The new route is usually PEX or copper, chosen for the house. Because it travels through accessible space, any future problem can be fixed without breaking concrete again. That is a real advantage in a home where the old lines have already failed once.

The cost of a reroute is patching drywall or a ceiling where the pipe passes. Where the layout makes that impractical, spot repair is the better fit. Our repiping page explains the larger version of this job.

When a slab leak needs a plumber tonight

Call now if water is surfacing through flooring, the meter is moving fast, or the house smells musty and the floors are warm. Water that pools under flooring can spread into walls and neighboring rooms, so waiting makes the damage bigger.

A slow slab leak that you only notice on the bill can usually wait for a same-day appointment when available. Either way, shut the main at night if no one is using water.

Water heater leaks near the slab edge

Not every wet floor is a pipe in the concrete. A water heater in a garage or closet can leak from a tank seam, a relief valve line or a fitting, and the water travels across the slab edge to a spot that looks like a slab leak.

We check the heater and its connections first when the wet area sits near it. If the tank itself is leaking, it needs replacing, and our water heater replacement crew handles that. If the heater is dry, the leak is in the pipes.

Pipe age in homes from the 1970s onward

Winter Springs became a city in 1959 as North Orlando and took its present name in 1972. Homes from the 1970s onward are common here, including the Tuscawilla development built around the 1971 country club.

Pipe age varies by subdivision and by whether the original lines were ever replaced, so ask what year your home was built. In an older house, a first slab leak can be a sign of other weak lines. That is why we test all lines before choosing spot repair over a reroute.

What a camera and sensors cannot do

A camera cannot see inside a slab, and acoustic gear works less well on plastic pipe or when water noise from a neighbor's line interferes. Sometimes the readings leave a small area of doubt rather than an exact point.

When that happens we say so and narrow it with more tests instead of cutting on a guess. If we cannot confirm a slab leak, leak detection in other parts of the house may find the real source.

What to do after the water is off

Stopping the water is only the first step. These actions protect the house and make the repair visit faster.

  • ✓Take photos of wet flooring, walls and any stains.
  • ✓Move furniture and rugs off the damp area.
  • ✓Run a fan or dehumidifier to slow mold.
  • ✓Note the meter reading and the time you shut the main.
  • ✓Write down which fixtures run hot and which do not.
  • ✓Keep the water heater off until we check it.

Insurance note

Wet flooring and drywall from a leak that appears suddenly are what insurance typically considers. Breaking the slab to reach the pipe, and the pipe that corroded, are often treated as a separate matter. Ask your insurer how your policy treats slab leaks before you start.

If you plan to file a claim, save your own photos and the meter readings you noted.
