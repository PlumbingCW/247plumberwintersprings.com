After a storm or flood: what to check in your plumbing

Heavy rain and rising water cause plumbing damage that does not show right away. Water can sit in wall cavities, sewers can back up into floor drains, and a water heater that sat in floodwater may have damaged controls or burners.

Do not trust a heater, outlet or appliance that was underwater. Turn off its power if you can do so from dry ground, then have it inspected before using it. Moisture meters and a thermal camera help us find water behind walls without tearing them open.

Before we arrive in Sanford

Storm and flood calls need quick, simple steps while a plumber is dispatched.

1

Shut the main valve

Turn the water main off if a line has broken or pipes inside are leaking. It stops supply water from adding to floodwater.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for flooded rooms from a dry panel only. If the panel itself is wet, stay away and call the electric utility.

3

Clear the area

Move valuables above floor level and stop running water so a sewer backup does not rise. Keep children and pets out of standing water.

Sewer backups when the ground is saturated

When the ground holds a lot of water, a cracked sewer pipe can take in groundwater and roots find the cracks. A line that normally copes can back up during a storm. Stopped-up floor drains, gurgling toilets and bubbling in the tub point to the main line.

Sewer line cleaning clears the blockage with a cable or a high-pressure water jet. A camera then shows whether the pipe is sound. If it is not, the next step is repair, and we explain it before any digging.

Storm-season checks for a Sanford house

A few minutes of checking before the rain can limit what the water does to the plumbing.

  • ✓Find your main shutoff and test that it turns
  • ✓Keep floor drains and cleanouts clear of leaves and mud
  • ✓Raise stored boxes off the floor near the water heater
  • ✓Look at the heater's base and relief pipe for stains
  • ✓Note the meter reading before and after a long storm
  • ✓Save the 24/7 number where everyone in the house can find it

Water in walls after the rain stops

Floodwater that recedes can leave damp drywall, insulation and framing behind. A plumber does not dry a house, but we can tell you whether a supply line or drain is still adding to the damage. A pressure test and meter check rule that in or out.

If a wall stays wet after the surface dries, that is a sign to call. Moisture readings and a thermal camera show the wet area without open demolition.

Water line repair on the Sanford side of the meter

Sanford draws from the Upper Floridan Aquifer and runs 2 treatment plants. Plant 1 uses aeration, and Plant 2 uses ozone plus granular activated carbon. Ask the city utility where its side of the line ends. Once you know which side the leak is on, we can plan the repair.

A leak between the meter and the house wastes water and can undermine soil. Water line repair means locating the break, then digging only as far as needed or running a new line. If the cause is a sewer problem instead, our sewer line repair work covers it.
