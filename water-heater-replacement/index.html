A water heater replacement means draining and removing the failed unit, setting a new one, reconnecting water, gas or power, venting and testing. It is different from a repair, which swaps a part inside a tank that still holds water. If you are not sure which one you need, a water heater repair visit can settle that first.

A flooding tank: what to do right now

Shut the cold valve on the pipe above the tank. If it will not turn, close the main water valve for the house. Cut power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and reachable without standing in water. Otherwise call. Turn a gas control to off.

Do not touch the relief valve, and do not try to move a full tank. A 40 or 50 gallon tank holds hundreds of pounds of water. Soaked drywall, insulation and flooring dry faster the sooner the standing water is gone.

Photograph the water line on the wall for your insurer before cleanup starts, and read the model and age from the data plate when you call.

Choosing the next heater: tank, hybrid or tankless

The old unit's fuel and size are a starting point, not a requirement. We lay out the choices with the trade-offs so you can decide.

Standard tank, same fuel

The simplest swap. Same connections, same venting, and usually the shortest job.

Hybrid heat pump tank

Uses a heat pump to move heat from surrounding air into the water. It needs room for airflow and a condensate drain.

Tankless

Heats water on demand and removes the tank leak risk, though gas supply, venting and flow needs have to be checked. See the tankless water heater page for details.

Switching fuel

Moving from gas to electric or heat pump means new wiring and removing the vent. Moving the other way means a gas line and a vent.

Hybrid heat pump options

A hybrid unit looks like a tall tank with a heat pump housing on top. It pulls warmth from the air around it, which cools and dries that space slightly. It works best in a garage or utility room with open airspace, not a closet with a slatted door.

Each model has its own clearance, filter and drain requirements. Moving from a gas tank to a heat pump unit is a real project, because the gas line is capped, the vent is removed and a dedicated electric circuit is needed. We check panel space and the drain path before quoting.

How a replacement day runs

The sequence is the same whether the old tank failed overnight or you planned the change.

  1. 1

    Stop the water and power

    We confirm the supply is shut, the breaker is off or the gas is closed, and drain what is left.

  2. 2

    Remove and haul the old unit

    The tank is disconnected, drained and carried out. Any floor damage is pointed out to you.

  3. 3

    Set and connect the new heater

    New flexible connectors, a relief valve with a discharge pipe and, where needed, an expansion tank.

  4. 4

    Vent or wire it

    Gas units get a vent check. Electric and heat pump units get a verified circuit.

  5. 5

    Fill, purge and test

    We fill the tank, purge air at a tap, check every joint for seepage and explain the settings.

What shapes the cost of replacing a water heater

The final price depends on the unit and the house. We explain each line before we start.

Unit type and size

A 40 gallon tank, a 50 gallon tank, a hybrid heat pump unit and a tankless heater sit at very different price points.

Fuel changes

Switching between gas and electric adds line, wiring and vent work beyond the heater itself.

Code items

Relief discharge piping, an expansion tank and venting must meet the Florida Building Code, Plumbing.

Location and access

Attic and tight closet installs take longer than garage floor swaps.

Permit and disposal

Permit fees, if required, and hauling the old tank are part of the job.

Insurance note

A tank that splits and ruins flooring is the sudden kind of loss a policy may cover. The rust that wore the tank out, and the cost of the new heater, usually fall outside it. Ask your insurer, and keep photos of the water damage for them.
