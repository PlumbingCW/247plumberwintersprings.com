How a slab leak gets confirmed

A hot spot on a floor or a meter that runs at night does not prove a slab leak. Here is how we separate it from other causes.

  1. 1

    Isolate the house

    We close the main and watch the meter. If it still moves when everything is off, water is leaking after the meter.

  2. 2

    Listen and scan

    Acoustic equipment hears water escaping under pressure, and a thermal camera shows a warm path where a hot line leaks.

  3. 3

    Test sections

    Pressure tests on the hot and cold lines show which one is losing pressure.

  4. 4

    Choose a fix

    Spot repair, a reroute through the attic or walls, or a full repipe. We explain the trade-offs before work starts.

When repiping beats a repair

A single repair makes sense when one section has failed and the rest tests sound. Repeated leaks in different spots tell a different story. If three pinholes have shown up in two years, the pipe is wearing out everywhere, and each patch buys less time.

Repiping replaces the supply lines with new material, usually through attic space and walls rather than the slab. Most of the cost is access and finish work. If the leak is under the floor, ask us how slab leak repair compares for your house before you decide.

A repipe also changes how a day in the house goes. Water is shut off for part of the work, and walls get small access holes that are patched afterward. We explain the schedule and what stays usable before work starts, so you can plan around it.
