Burst pipe repair in Winter Springs, FL means stopping the flow, cutting out the split or cracked section and joining in new pipe that holds full line pressure. A pipe that is pouring water is an emergency, and our 24/7 dispatch answers at any hour.

While you wait, turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops, then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines. Cut power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and reachable without standing in water. Otherwise call. Photograph the damage for your insurer.

Water is pouring: do this before we arrive

The first few minutes decide how much of the house gets wet. Move in this order.

  1. 1

    Close the main valve

    It is usually at the front of the house, near the hose bib. Turn it fully. Do not force the valve at the meter.

  2. 2

    Drain the lines

    Open a low faucet and flush a toilet so water already in the pipes does not keep leaking.

  3. 3

    Shut off the water heater

    Set a gas heater to pilot. For an electric heater, cut power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and reachable without standing in water. Otherwise call. An empty tank can damage elements.

  4. 4

    Protect the area

    Move furniture, lift rugs, set out buckets and mop standing water so it cannot soak into flooring.

  5. 5

    Call us

    Tell us where the water is coming from and whether the main closed. That shapes what we bring.

Cracked and broken pipe repair

Pipes crack for a few common reasons. Old fittings corrode and let go, a nail or screw punctures a line behind drywall, water pressure surges stress a weak joint, or a section that was kinked at installation finally gives out. Each leaves a different kind of break.

We find the full extent of the damage before cutting. A split in copper may be a few inches long, but the pipe on either side may be thinned. We trim back to sound material, join in new pipe with proper fittings and pressure test before closing the wall.

If the same line keeps failing, a spot patch is not the answer. We will tell you when a larger section, or a repiping project, makes more sense.

Broken or stuck supply valves

A supply stop under a sink or behind a toilet that snaps, seeps or will not close can flood a cabinet fast. These small valves are made of soft brass or plastic, and a nut that turns too hard cracks them.

If the stop will not shut, close the main and call. We replace the valve with a quarter-turn stop and a new braided supply line, and check the connection for leaks under pressure. The repair itself is small, yet a failed valve can empty a supply line into a cabinet in minutes.

Why pipes burst in a warm climate

Freezes can still split exposed lines on a cold night, but most failures here come from the causes below.

High water pressure

Pressure above what fittings are rated for stresses joints. A pressure gauge on a hose bib shows where yours sits.

Water hammer

A fast-closing valve sends a shock wave through the line that can crack an old joint.

Corrosion and age

Copper pits and galvanized steel rusts from inside, thinning the wall until it splits.

Damage during other work

A nail, screw or shifted pipe from a renovation can nick a line that fails months later.

Cold nights

On a cold night, an exposed line or outdoor hose bib can freeze and split before it thaws.

Copper, drain and leaking pipe repair

Not every failure is a dramatic burst. A weeping joint, a pinhole in copper or a cracked drain pipe under a sink all leak slowly but still need repair, because the wetness rots cabinets and floors.

Copper pinholes often show green or white crust. A drain pipe leak is usually seen at a coupling or trap. We pressure test the supply line and run water through the drain to confirm where the drip starts, then replace the damaged part. For leaks we cannot see, our leak detection service finds the source.

When the shutoff at the house will not close

If the shutoff at your house will not close, do not force the valve at the meter. A valve that gives way under a wrench can turn a leak into a flood.

The City of Winter Springs lists 24-hour utility emergencies at (407) 327-2669. If water is running in the street or leaking at the meter, that number is the one to call. If the pipe broke inside the house, call us and we will repair it.

What changes the price of a burst pipe repair

We give a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that move it.

Pipe material and size

Copper and larger diameters cost more in parts and time than short PEX runs.

Location of the break

A pipe in an open attic is easier than one in a finished wall or a slab.

Length of damage

One split joint is a quick fix. Several inches of corroded pipe means a longer section.

Wall and ceiling access

More drywall to cut and patch adds time.

After-hours call

Ask whether the hour of the call affects the price. You hear the price before work starts.

Insurance note

Flooring and drywall soaked when a pipe splits suddenly are the kind of accidental damage insurance typically considers. A pipe that failed from slow corrosion, and the cost of replacing that pipe, are usually treated differently. Ask your insurer, and keep your own photos of the damage.

If the pipe is part of a larger problem, we say so before work starts.

If the pipe is gone and you have no water

When a section is removed or the main is shut, the house has no water until the repair is done. We usually restore service the same visit when the repair is straightforward, though we cannot promise a time before we see the damage.

If the break is bigger than expected, we may need to replace more pipe. We tell you what we found, what we recommend and what it will cost before we proceed. For lines running from the meter to your house, see our water line repair page.
