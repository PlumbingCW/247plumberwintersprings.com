Water line repair in Winter Springs, FL covers the buried pipe that carries water from the city meter into your house. When it breaks, you can lose pressure, flood a yard or see a very large bill. Our 24/7 dispatch handles those calls.

While you wait, close the shutoff valve at the house. If water is still bubbling up outside, the leak is on the meter side of that valve, so note where the water is surfacing and keep children and pets away from soft, muddy ground.

Soggy lawn or weak pressure: what a leaking service line looks like

The service line is out of sight, so you read it by its effects. These are the common ones.

A wet patch in a dry stretch

Grass that stays green and soggy between the meter and the house often sits over a leak.

Pressure drops at every tap

A broken line loses pressure before it reaches fixtures, so showers and sinks all run weak.

A meter that moves with the house closed

If the meter turns after you shut the house valve, the leak is on the buried line.

Sinking pavement or a depression

Water washing soil from under a walkway or driveway can leave a dip.

Hissing near the meter box

A faint sound near the meter or the front hose bib can mark a failing joint.

Finding and repairing a water service line leak

We start with the meter test. Turn everything off, close the house valve and watch the meter. A moving dial means the leak is buried between the meter and the house. A still dial sends us inside.

Next we pinpoint the break with listening gear along the pipe route and a pressure check on the line. The goal is to dig one hole at the right place, not trench the whole yard.

Most repairs replace the damaged section with new pipe and fittings. If the line is old and failing in several places, replacing the full run can make more sense than repeated digs. Our leak detection visit often comes first when the location is unclear.

What the repair day looks like

A buried line repair has a predictable order. Knowing it helps you plan around the work.

  1. 1

    Confirm the line and mark the route

    We repeat the meter test, then mark the pipe path so the dig stays small.

  2. 2

    Check utility marks

    Digging waits until an 811 ticket is filed and the buried lines are marked.

  3. 3

    Expose and repair

    We dig at the leak, cut out the damaged pipe and join in new pipe with proper fittings.

  4. 4

    Pressure test

    We restore water and check the new joints under full pressure before backfilling.

  5. 5

    Backfill and review

    We close the trench and walk you through what was found. A split inside the house is handled as burst pipe repair instead.

When a water line leak is an emergency

Treat it as urgent when water is pooling against the house, flowing across the street, undermining a driveway or running with a force that you cannot slow. Shut the house valve and call our 24/7 line.

A slow wet patch can wait for a same-day appointment when available, but do not let it sit for weeks. Leaking water softens the ground and speeds up pipe failure.

Low water pressure that is not the line

Low pressure does not always mean a broken line. A partly closed main valve, a failing pressure regulator, clogged aerators or galvanized pipe that is scaled inside can all choke flow.

We check pressure at a hose bib with a gauge, then compare it at several fixtures. If every tap is low, the problem is near the source. If only one fixture is weak, the aerator, cartridge or supply stop is usually to blame.

Pressure that swings high and low is a regulator problem. Pressure that dropped slowly over years is more likely corroded pipe, which may call for repiping rather than a service line fix.

Winter Springs Local

The city side, the house side and 811

The City of Winter Springs utility draws groundwater from the Floridan Aquifer through 8 wells and treats it at three plants. Your service line is the last stretch of that network, and the city utility division can tell you where its responsibility ends.

Details on the utility are on the city's utility page. For utility emergencies, the city lists a 24-hour number of (407) 327-2669. Before any digging, an 811 call is needed at least two full business days ahead, or a ticket can be filed at Sunshine 811 any time. The property owner or contractor files the 811 ticket. Ask us on the call.

Water main replacement, outdoor leaks and wells

We repair the service line from the meter to the house. A water main is the city's pipe under the street, and work on it belongs to the utility. If you suspect a main break, call the city.

Outdoor leaks at hose bibs, irrigation connections or a backyard spigot are quicker repairs, and we handle them with the same meter test.

Well pressure tanks are different. If your home has a private well, a pressure tank or pump is a specialty we do not cover, and a well contractor is the right call.

What affects the price of a water line repair

We give a clear price before work starts. These factors drive it.

Depth and length of the line

A shallow short section is quicker than a long run buried deep.

What is above the pipe

Grass is simple. A driveway, patio or tree roots add cutting, hauling and restoration.

Pipe material

Copper, PEX and polyethylene pipe each have different material and labor costs.

Number of leaks

Multiple breaks tilt the job toward replacing a longer section.

Time of the call

Ask whether the hour of the call affects the price. You hear the price before work starts.

Insurance note

If a buried line fails suddenly and floods part of the house, insurance typically covers that accidental damage. Many policies do not cover a slow underground leak, wear or the repair of the pipe itself. Some offer separate service line coverage, so ask your insurer.

If your insurer asks what failed, tell them the meter test result and where the leak sat in the line.
