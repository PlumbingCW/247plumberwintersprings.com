Sewer Inspection in Winter Springs, FL

A camera on a flexible cable shows what is inside the pipe, and a locator marks where it sits underground.

A sewer inspection in Winter Springs, FL is a video look inside your sewer line, taken with a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable. It shows cracks, roots, sags and blockages, and a locator above ground marks the depth and position of anything it finds.

People book one for a recurring clog, a wet patch in the yard, a bad smell, or before buying a house. Each of those reasons asks a slightly different question, and the camera answers it with a picture instead of a guess.

What the camera can and cannot see

The camera head carries lights and sends live video to a monitor. We push it from a cleanout or a pulled toilet and follow the pipe toward the street connection. The cable has a distance counter, so a defect can be tied to a measured spot.

It shows the pipe material, joints, cracks, root growth, scale, standing water at a low spot, and objects that should not be there. It also shows where a branch joins the main.

It cannot see through a full blockage, and it cannot see inside a slab or past a collapsed section. When that happens, we clear the line first or tell you what the limit is. We do not guess about what we cannot see.

How the inspection runs

A typical visit follows these steps.

  1. 1

    Talk through the symptoms

    We ask which fixtures are slow, when it started, and whether anything was cleared before.

  2. 2

    Find an entry point

    We use a cleanout when one exists. If not, we open a vent or pull a toilet.

  3. 3

    Run the camera

    We record the run and note the distance of each defect.

  4. 4

    Locate and mark

    A locator above ground picks up the camera signal, so the depth and position can be marked on the lawn.

  5. 5

    Explain the findings

    You see the footage and hear what is minor and what needs work.

Suspecting a sewer line leak

A sewer line leak is hard to see because the pipe is not under pressure. Wastewater seeps into the soil through a crack or open joint. Signs include a patch of lush or soggy grass, a sewer smell outdoors, a sinkhole or sunken spot, and drains that clog for no clear reason.

The camera can show a crack or a joint with soil visible behind it, and standing water in a belly can point to a sag. If the problem is inside a slab, we can mark the pipe's path but cannot see through concrete.

When the signs point to a clean water line rather than the sewer, leak detection is the right service, and we say so before charging for a camera run.

Reading the footage: what common defects mean

Footage is only useful if someone explains it. These are the findings we see most and what each one usually means.

Root intrusion

Fine white threads or a thick mat at a joint mean roots have found a gap. Cutting them gives temporary relief, and sealing the joint is the lasting fix.

Belly or sag

Standing water along a stretch of pipe means the grade has settled. Solids collect there and cause repeat clogs.

Offset joint

Two pipe ends that do not line up leave a ledge that catches paper. It can also let soil in.

Scale and grease

A rough, narrowed wall points to buildup, which hydro jetting can wash off if the pipe is sound.

Cracks or missing pieces

These need repair, since cleaning cannot restore a broken wall.

Before you buy a house

A standard home inspection does not usually include running a camera through the sewer line. If the lateral is cracked or full of roots, the seller may not know, and the repair can become yours after closing.

A camera inspection during your contract's inspection period shows you the pipe's condition and any defects before you commit. Ask the seller for access to a cleanout, and plan time for the visit.

We tell you what the footage shows and what it does not. We do not give a legal opinion or promise how long the pipe will last. If the camera finds a defect, our page on sewer line repair explains the options.

Winter Springs Local

Winter Springs homes, lift stations and what to ask

Winter Springs was incorporated in 1959 and homes from the 1970s onward are common, so pipe age varies by subdivision. Ask what year your home was built, and mention it when you book, because it helps us anticipate what we might see.

The city's utility division lists 43 lift stations in the wastewater system, along with 112 miles of gravity mains and 24 miles of force mains. A lift station is a pump that moves wastewater uphill when gravity will not do it. If a camera shows clear flow in your lateral and the trouble remains, ask the city Utility Division where city responsibility ends and whether they have seen issues on your street.

What a sewer inspection costs depends on

We give a clear price before work starts. These factors move the number.

Access to the line

A cleanout is the easy case. Pulling a toilet or opening a vent takes more time.

Length of the run

A short lateral is quick. A long run to the street takes longer.

Cleaning before the camera

If the pipe is too blocked to scope, it has to be cleared first, which is priced separately.

Locating and marking

Tracing the pipe route and depth above ground adds time.

Recording the run

A recorded run for a buyer or lender takes extra time on site.

Insurance note

An inspection is a diagnostic step, and insurance does not typically pay for it or for wear and root intrusion. It covers sudden accidental damage. If you are filing a claim, ask your insurer what they require.

If the camera finds nothing wrong, you will know the pipe is sound on the day it was inspected, and that is useful too.
