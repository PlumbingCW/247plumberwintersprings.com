Garbage disposal repair in Winter Springs, FL starts with working out whether the unit is jammed, burned out, leaking from a seal, or only blocked downstream in the drain. Each has a different fix, and sometimes a replacement costs less than chasing a failing motor.

The sound and the symptom point to the cause before anyone removes a part.

Freeing a jammed disposal safely

A jam is the most common disposal call, and most homeowners can clear one if the unit is truly powered down. Stop and call if you are unsure at any step.

  1. 1

    Kill the power

    Switch off the wall switch, then unplug the unit from the outlet under the sink or turn off its breaker.

  2. 2

    Find the hex socket

    Most units have a small socket in the center of the underside. A hex key turned back and forth frees the flywheel.

  3. 3

    Look with a flashlight

    Remove any visible item with tongs or pliers. Never use your fingers.

  4. 4

    Press the reset

    The red button on the bottom pops out when the motor overheats. Press it once, restore power, and run cold water to test.

A blocked kitchen sink is often not the disposal

Standing water in the sink with the disposal running means the problem is usually downstream. Grease, coffee grounds and starchy food like pasta or rice settle in the P-trap and the pipe beyond it. The motor spins fine, but there is nowhere for the slurry to go.

A plumber checks by disconnecting the trap and looking at what is inside. If the blockage is deeper, a cable machine clears the branch line. For recurring kitchen clogs, see drain cleaning, because a disposal does not cure a narrowed line.

Putting grease down the drain, even with hot water, is how most kitchen lines narrow. It cools a few feet down and coats the pipe wall.

Repair, reconnect or replace

Not every disposal is worth repairing. We tell you where the line is.

Repair

A jam, a loose mount or a leaking drain flange are inexpensive repairs, and the unit usually runs for years afterward.

Reconnect

If you are redoing a sink, we disconnect and reattach the disposal, trap and dishwasher drain line so nothing leaks when you finish.

Replace

A burned-out motor, rusted shell, or a unit that trips the reset repeatedly is cheaper to swap than to rebuild.

Dishwasher connection

The dishwasher drain hose should loop high or run through an air gap so wastewater cannot flow back into the dishwasher. We check it whenever we touch the disposal.

Putting in a new disposal

A replacement starts at the sink flange, the ring that holds the unit to the sink bowl. We remove the old mounting assembly, clean off old putty, seat the new flange in fresh plumber's putty, and lock the mounting ring underneath. The disposal then hangs from that ring and twists into place.

Next come the connections. The discharge tube joins the trap with a gasket, the dishwasher hose attaches to a knockout port that must be punched out first if a dishwasher is present, and the power cord or hardwired lead goes to a junction box. Forgetting the knockout plug is the classic mistake, and it leaves the dishwasher draining into a blocked port.

Fill the sink, release the water and watch every joint for seepage. We also run the unit with cold water for a minute to confirm it spins freely and the reset stays in. If the sink needs other changes, we can pair this with faucet repair in the same visit.

Kitchen sink odors and city water

Winter Springs draws groundwater from the Floridan Aquifer, and the city utility treats it by aeration to remove hydrogen sulfide before chlorine is added. A rotten-egg smell from the hot tap alone points to the water heater, not the disposal.

If the smell comes only from the kitchen drain and is worst after the disposal runs, food residue on the splash guard and inside the chamber is the usual source. A brush under the rubber baffle and a rinse with dish soap handle most of it. If the smell persists, call us and we will check the trap.

What a disposal repair depends on

We quote a clear price before work starts. These items shape it.

Repair or new unit

A jam clear is a short visit. A new disposal adds the unit, mounting parts and an electrical connection.

Horsepower and model

Larger motors and sound-insulated models cost more than basic ones.

Electrical and switch

A unit wired to a switch or plug needs checking. If the outlet is faulty, an electrician may be needed.

Drain condition

A badly clogged trap or branch adds drain work to the visit.

Time of day

Ask whether the hour of the call affects the price. You hear the price before work starts.

Insurance note

A seal that fails and floods the cabinet floor is sudden damage a policy may cover. A motor that burns out after years of use is wear, and the disposal itself is usually not covered. Ask your insurer about your policy.
