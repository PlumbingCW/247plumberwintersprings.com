Plumbing installation in Winter Springs, FL means putting in new fixtures, supply lines, drains, pumps and gas piping so they work, pass inspection where required, and do not leak behind a wall. Our licensed plumbers install the work, test it under pressure, and explain what we found.

Installation jobs are won or lost in the planning. Pipe size, slope, venting and shutoff placement are set before any fixture arrives, and the permit question is raised on day one rather than after the drywall goes up.

Smell gas? Leave first, then call

If you smell gas, hear a hiss near a gas line, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the building at once. Do not switch lights on or off, use a phone inside, or start a car in the garage. Call 911 or your gas utility from outside, before you call a plumber.

A plumber can inspect and repair gas piping after the utility has made the area safe, but we do not promise a gas response time, and a gas leak is never a job to wait out. Once it is safe, we can pressure-test the line, find the failed fitting and replace it.

Gas line inspection and new gas piping

Adding a gas dryer, range, outdoor grill line or tankless water heater means running new gas pipe from an existing branch. The pipe has to be sized for the total demand of the appliances on that branch and the length of the run, and the appliance rating plate shows the input it needs.

Before connecting, we pressure-test the new section with air and check every joint with a leak-detecting solution. Permit rules vary, so ask the Winter Springs building department about what your job needs. We can tell you what documents the city form asks for, including the number of plumbing fixtures and a licensed contractor and qualifier.

Backflow testing and assemblies

A backflow device keeps water from moving backward into the drinking supply. The city cross-connection control manual, Ordinance No. 493, adopted July 23, 1990, requires devices with test cocks to be tested once a year. Only certified device testers are accepted by the city utility department.

Lawn sprinkler systems must have an AVB, PVB or DCVA, and a DCVA is used on fire sprinkler lines. Hospitals need an RP device on main service connections. The customer pays for testing and repairs, and results go to the city Utility Department.

We can install a new assembly or replace a failed one, and a certified tester must perform the annual test. Ask the city utility which test form it wants. See commercial plumbing if the device sits on a business property.

New construction and remodel rough-ins

Rough-in is the plumbing that goes in before walls and slab are finished. On new construction it covers underground drains, supply lines and fixture stub-outs. The city permit form asks for the number of plumbing fixtures, which relates to Florida Building Code, Plumbing, Sec. 403.

On a remodel, the harder question is what already exists. Old drains may need venting changes, and a bathroom moved across the house means new waste lines and a new vent path. We assess it before pricing.

Before you dig in Winter Springs

Florida law, Chapter 556, asks anyone digging to call 811 at least two full business days before starting. Online tickets can be filed any hour at sunshine811.com. Utility lines run under driveways, lawns and sidewalks, and hitting one with an excavator or a trencher turns a plumbing job into an outage.

The city building department sits at 1126 East State Road 434, and its application asks for plumbing fixture counts and a contractor license number. If your project is a water heater or a pump swap, permit rules vary, so ask the Winter Springs building department before work starts.

Insurance note

A planned installation is not an insured loss, so the new fixture or line is your cost. If a fitting fails later and floods a room, that sudden damage may be covered, though the failed part itself usually is not. Ask your insurer.
